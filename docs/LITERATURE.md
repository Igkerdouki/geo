# Literature review: 51 papers

Every paper below was checked against PubMed, Crossref or the publisher (DOI or PMID). Numbers come from abstracts or
full texts. ⭐ = why it matters for Geo. Strength: **S** strong · **M** moderate · **W** weak.

All of this is observational evidence: say "associated with", never "causes".

## What the papers say together

1. **The harm is real and local.** Pesticide use within 0.5–2 km of home is linked to autism, lower IQ, Parkinson's,
   childhood leukemia and worse birth outcomes (1–10), concentrated in the most-exposed tail (6) and in Latino
   children (11).
2. **Medicine doesn't see it.** Clinicians rarely take exposure histories (32, 33), feel unprepared (19), miss and
   underreport pesticide illness (18, 21), and almost never discuss air pollution with patients (31).
3. **The method exists but is manual.** Researchers link addresses to pesticide records by hand (42–47) and ask for
   address histories over time (34). Geo automates this at the point of care.
4. **Design matters more than data.** Flags without a next step are ignored (37), repeats cause fatigue (38, Graff
   Zivin), broadcast alerts help only asthma (30), and exact maps expose patients (41).
5. **Tools make AI trustworthy.** Agents with data tools are far more accurate than agents without (40), but still need
   validated tool calls (39, EHR-MCP).

## Pitch stats (strongest, filtered)
- Doctors record a patient's age ~99% of the time but their job only **27.8%** (Politi 2004).
- Residents of farm-heavy California counties: **~27× higher** rate of drift-related pesticide illness; fumigants cause
  **45%** (Lee 2011).
- Highest prenatal pesticide exposure → **7 IQ points lower** at age 7 (Bouchard 2011, Salinas Valley).
- Hispanic children **91% more likely** to attend schools with the most pesticide use nearby (CDPH 2014).
- Asthma ER visits **+112%** at peak wildfire smoke in San Diego, **+243%** in infants (Hutchinson 2018).
- Only **3%** of adults ever discussed air pollution with a clinician (Mirabelli 2018).
- AI agent with tools: **87%** correct vs **30%** without (Ferber 2025).

## Honest caveats
- All health studies are observational. Say "associated with."
- PUR locates applications to ~1 square mile and doesn't record time of day. Exposure is estimated, not measured.
- Plume models are approximate (near-field good, far-field overpredicts).
- No study yet shows that patient-level calls reduce ER visits. Treat it as a hypothesis and measure it.
- Geo is a **screening and awareness tool**, not a dose diagnosis.
